Finding the path behind a firefly

A firefly is one sample. crust light-paths re-renders a few pixels exactly as the render did and lists the light paths their samples took — which expression, through which light, from which sample — so the path behind a bright pixel has a name you can act on.

From a bright pixel to its path

The loop has three steps: find the pixel, inspect the region around it, and author the path it names as an AOV to see it over the whole frame.

1. Find the pixel

Open the render in an image viewer and read the coordinates of the bright pixel, from the image's top-left corner (as --region and an EXR data window count them). In a Claude Desktop session, probe gives the same: the pixel's value, its prim and its material. crust diagnostic says whether fireflies matter (its firefly_energy finding); this page is about which path they are.

2. Inspect the region

Ask for a region of at most 100 pixels around it, at the sample count the render used:

crust light-paths -i shot.usda -s 256 --region 618,331,628,341

The region is rendered with the render's own settings, so its samples are the frame's: every pixel in the report's pixels is the pixel the render wrote, bit for bit, and the records are the samples of the firefly you saw, not a lookalike. Two cases change that, and the report says so in reproduces_frame_reason: path guiding (it trains on the region alone) and adaptive sampling's neighbour hold (a pixel at the region's edge can stop earlier than in the frame). Render with crust:adaptiveNeighbourTolerance = -1, or at a sample count below the adaptive minimum, when you need the exact samples.

The Markdown summary opens with one sentence:

The top group carries 8% of the region's luminance and 98% of its second moment:
C<RD'diffuse'><RS'specular'>L via /lights/bulb, in 12 of 25600 samples (13610 records in 10 groups).

Read it as: one path — a diffuse bounce off the floor, a mirror ball, then the bulb — takes 12 samples in 25 600 and holds almost all the variance. That is the firefly: a caustic, which light sampling cannot reach through the mirror. Its brightest record has a luminance of 56, where the floor's direct light (C<RD'diffuse'>L, 78% of the region's light) never exceeds 0.15.

3. See it over the frame

Every expression in the report is written in crust's light path expression grammar, the light named by its crust:light:lpeTag when it has one. Pasted into an lpe RenderVar, it routes the same paths over the whole frame:

def RenderVar "caustic"
{
    uniform token dataType = "color3f"
    uniform string sourceName = "C<RD'diffuse'><RS'specular'>L"
    uniform token sourceType = "lpe"
}

Render with it and look at the channel: where else the path lands, and how much of the image it is.

What the report holds

--json PATH (or - for stdout) writes crust-light-paths/1. The order is the order to read it in:

  • summary: the sentence above.
  • groups, best first by second_moment_share, then luma_share. Each has its key, expression and light; luma_share (its share of the region's light, as the pixels average it) and luma_share_clamped (the same after the indirect clamp, over the same total); second_moment_share (its share of the sum of every sample's squared luminance: the noise); hit_rate (the fraction of samples that took it); max_luma; and top_records, its paths among records. --group-by picks the key: expr+light (default), expr, light or none.
  • records, the --top brightest light paths (32 by default, at most 1000): luma, contribution and contribution_clamped (RGB), expression, lobe_split (each vertex's share per lobe), label_ambiguous, light, pixel, coordinates (where in the pixel the sample was taken), sample, depth, camera, and its group.
  • truncated: how many paths were kept, of how many.
  • pixels: each pixel's beauty and sample count.
  • consistency: how exactly the paths add back up to the pixels (max_relative_error) and to each sample (max_sample_relative_error) — about 1e-7, rounding. A path is one term of the render's own estimate, so they partition it: nothing is counted twice.
  • clamp_removed_share, ambiguous_luma_share, and hints.

Hints

Each hint is a code, the group it is about, the rule that fired it and a next step:

codefires whenmeans
rare_bright_patha group's hit_rate < 1e-3 and second_moment_share > 0.5a firefly: few samples, most of the noise
single_path_dominatesa group's luma_share > 0.8the region's light is this one path
clamp_hides_patha group's luma_share_clamped < half its luma_sharethe clamp removes most of the path: inspect with --indirect-clamp 0 to see what it carries
labels_ambiguousambiguous_luma_share > 0.2see below

A hint never changes a setting for you; the clamp and the sample count trade the picture for the noise, and are yours to decide.

How a path is named

A path's expression names, at each vertex, the lobe carrying most of that vertex's light toward the path's direction: crust's materials sample one lobe at a time, but every lobe contributes to every direction, so the lobe that was drawn is often not the one that carried the light. Each vertex's split between its lobes is listed in lobe_split, and a path whose named lobe carries less than 60% somewhere is label_ambiguous. ambiguous_luma_share says how much of the region's light that is. Grouping by expression is exact for the rest; for the exact split of an ambiguous one, author one expression per lobe — the AOVs split every sample by lobe.

Limitations

  • At most 100 pixels per inspection, and one camera.
  • prims is null for every path, and --group-by expr+prims+light is refused, until crust can resolve a hit to its prim.
  • Under path guiding, and under adaptive sampling's neighbour hold, the region's samples are the scene's paths but not the frame's samples.
  • The hint thresholds are first guesses, to be tuned on real scenes.
